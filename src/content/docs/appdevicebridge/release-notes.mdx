## 1.2 - TBD

<RN type="feature">**Printing bridges.** Two new packages, one for each of Shiny 5.9's printing models. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Printers` adds `/_bridge/printers` for ESC/POS and TSPL receipt and label printers, backed by Shiny.Printers. It scans Bluetooth LE for known printer models and mDNS for network printers, connects to one at a time, and prints receipts the page builds as a list of elements: text, alignment, size, barcodes, QR codes, images (a PNG or JPEG, scaled to the printer's width), cuts and raw bytes. `POST printers/render` turns the same receipt into a PDF. `printers.disconnected` fires when a printer goes away. A printer named by address must be an IP address on the local network and on a raw printer port (9100–9102, configurable with `PrintersBridgeOptions.NetworkPorts`), because a receipt can carry raw bytes. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Printing` adds `/_bridge/printing` for the operating system's own printing, backed by Shiny.Printing: print a PDF, an image, HTML or an http(s) page through the print dialog, or silently to an installed printer where the platform allows it. A large PDF or image goes as the raw body of `POST printing/print/pdf` or `print/image` (`PrintPdfAsync` / `PrintImageAsync` taking a `Stream`, or `printPdf` / `printImage` taking a `Blob`), streamed to a temporary file, up to `PrintingBridgeOptions.MaxFileBytes` (512 MB). The JSON route is capped at `MaxJsonBytes` (16 MB). `POST printing/html-to-pdf` (`HtmlToPdfAsync`, or `htmlToPdf` in TypeScript) lays HTML out on pages and returns the PDF with no print UI, on iOS, Mac Catalyst and Android, where `capabilities.htmlToPdf` is true; elsewhere it answers `501`. Built on Shiny 5.9.0-beta-0004. Register them with `bridge.AddPrintersBridge()` and `bridge.AddPrintingBridge()`. The clients are `IPrintersBridge` (with a `PrintElements` builder) and `IPrintingBridge`, or `PrintersBridge` and `PrintingBridge` in TypeScript. Network printers work on every platform. Bluetooth LE printers work on Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS and Windows, and on Linux with Shiny.BluetoothLE.Linux registered. OS printing uses PrintManager on Android, AirPrint on iOS and Mac Catalyst, the spooler on Windows and CUPS on Linux and macOS. Content a platform can't print, such as HTML on Windows and CUPS, answers `501`, as does rendering on Linux without SkiaSharp.NativeAssets.Linux. See [Printing](/appdevicebridge/printing/).</RN>

## 1.1 - September 30, 2026

<RN type="breaking">**`Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Locations` is split into `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps` and `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Geofencing`**, following Shiny.Locations' split into Shiny.Gps and Shiny.Geofencing. `.Gps` has `AddGpsBridge()` and `AddMotionActivityBridge()`, and `.Geofencing` has `AddGeofenceBridge()`. Reference only the one you use. `AddLocationBridges()` is removed: call `AddGpsBridge().AddGeofenceBridge()` instead. The clients move with them. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps.Client` has `IGpsBridge`, `IMotionBridge` and `GpsJsonContext`, and `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Geofencing.Client` has `IGeofencesBridge` and `GeofencingJsonContext`. `LocationsJsonContext` is gone. `LocationAccessResult` becomes `GpsAccessResult`, `MotionAccessResult` and `GeofenceAccessResult`, with the same `{ "access": … }` JSON. In TypeScript, the `locations` module becomes `gps` and `geofencing`, still exported from the package root. Routes, events and handler names are unchanged. Platforms are unchanged: GPS and geofencing on Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows, motion activity on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst, and `501` elsewhere.</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Beacons bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Beacons`, backed by Shiny.Beacons, adds `/_bridge/beacons`. Ranging reports the iBeacons in a region with estimated distances as `beacons.ranged`. Monitoring reports region enter and exit in the background and across restarts, as `beacons.region` and the `beacon` handler in background.js. Scanning reports Eddystone UID, URL, TLM and EID frames as `beacons.eddystone`, and broadcasting advertises the device as an iBeacon, Eddystone-UID or Eddystone-URL beacon. Register it with `bridge.AddBeaconsBridge()`, or pass `BeaconFeatures` flags for only some features. The client is `IBeaconsBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Beacons.Client`, or `BeaconsBridge` in TypeScript. Ranging and scans answer `409 not_listening` until their event is listened to, and stop with the last listener. Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows have every feature. macOS has Eddystone and broadcasting, and answers `501` for iBeacon ranging and monitoring, which CoreLocation lacks there. Linux answers `501`. See [Bridges](/appdevicebridge/bridges/).</RN>
