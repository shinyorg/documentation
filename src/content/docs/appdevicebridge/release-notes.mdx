## 1.2 - TBD

<RN type="feature">**Document geofencing bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.DocumentGeofencing`, backed by Shiny.DocumentDb.Geofencing 14.1, adds `/_bridge/documentgeofences`. Regions are documents in a Shiny.DocumentDb store: polygons matched by containment, or points and shapes matched within a distance, with no OS limit on how many. Background GPS evaluates each reading against every region set. The app registers the region sets in C# with `bridge.AddDocumentGeofenceBridge(cfg => cfg.AddRegionSet<T>(…))`. The page checks status, requests access, starts and stops monitoring, and reads the region the device is in for each set (`GET documentgeofences/current`). Entering or leaving a region arrives as `documentgeofence.change`, or at background.js's `documentgeofence` handler when no page is open. Each change carries the region document, serialized with the store's own JSON options and never by reflection. Set `o => o.UseDelegate<T>()` to handle changes in C# as well, and `o.RegionSerializerOptions` when the regions live in a keyed store. Starting answers `409 geofence_refused` without background location permission, and `501 spatial_not_supported` against a store without spatial queries. Monitoring shares Shiny.Gps' one listener with the GPS bridge. The client is `IDocumentGeofencesBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.DocumentGeofencing.Client`, or `DocumentGeofencesBridge` in TypeScript. Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst; every other platform answers `501`. See [Document Geofencing](/appdevicebridge/document-geofencing/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Reverse geocoding bridge.** `AddGeocodingBridge()` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps` adds `GET /_bridge/geocoding/reverse?latitude=&longitude=`, which returns the placemarks at a position, most relevant first. It uses MapKit / CoreLocation on iOS and Mac Catalyst and `android.location.Geocoder` on Android. Windows, Linux, macOS and Android devices without a geocoding backend use OpenStreetMap's Nominatim (Shiny.Gps 5.9.0-beta-0009), so it works on every platform; register `AddGeocoding(o => …)` first to point it at your own server. A position off the map answers `400`, and an unreachable geocoder `503 geocoder_unavailable`. The client is `IGeocodingBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps.Client`, or `GeocodingBridge` in TypeScript. See [GPS & Motion](/appdevicebridge/gps/#reverse-geocoding).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**In-app purchases bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.InAppPurchases`, backed by Shiny.Mobile.InAppPurchases 5.9, adds `/_bridge/purchases` for consumables, non-consumables and auto-renewable subscriptions through the App Store (StoreKit 2) and Google Play Billing. The page loads localized products with their subscription offers, opens the store's purchase sheet (`Success`, `Pending`, `Cancelled` and `AlreadyOwned` are results, not errors), and reads entitlements and unfinished purchases. It can also restore purchases and open subscription management. `POST purchases/transactions/{transactionId}/finish` with `{ consume }` finishes a purchase once the app's server has verified `verificationData` and granted it. The bridge looks the purchase up in the store by its transaction id instead of trusting a record from the page, and answers `404 purchase_not_found` when there is none. Ask to Buy approvals, renewals, refunds and purchases on other devices arrive as `purchases.updated`, in the page or in background.js when no page is open. Store failures answer with codes the page can switch on, such as `503 store_unavailable`, `403 not_allowed`, `404 product_not_found` and `409 invalid_state`. Register it with `bridge.AddInAppPurchasesBridge()`; an app's own `IPurchaseDelegate` keeps running beside the bridge's. The client is `IInAppPurchasesBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.InAppPurchases.Client`, or `InAppPurchasesBridge` in TypeScript. iOS 15+ and Android; every other platform answers `501`. See [In-App Purchases](/appdevicebridge/inapppurchases/).</RN>
<RN type="enhancement">**Shiny 5.9.0-beta-0009.** Every Shiny-backed bridge now builds on Shiny 5.9.0-beta-0009, up from beta-0004.</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Printing bridges.** Two new packages, one for each of Shiny 5.9's printing models. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Printers` adds `/_bridge/printers` for ESC/POS and TSPL receipt and label printers, backed by Shiny.Printers. It scans Bluetooth LE for known printer models and mDNS for network printers, connects to one at a time, and prints receipts the page builds as a list of elements: text, alignment, size, barcodes, QR codes, images (a PNG or JPEG, scaled to the printer's width), cuts and raw bytes. `POST printers/render` turns the same receipt into a PDF. `printers.disconnected` fires when a printer goes away. A printer named by address must be an IP address on the local network and on a raw printer port (9100–9102, configurable with `PrintersBridgeOptions.NetworkPorts`), because a receipt can carry raw bytes. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Printing` adds `/_bridge/printing` for the operating system's own printing, backed by Shiny.Printing: print a PDF, an image, HTML or an http(s) page through the print dialog, or silently to an installed printer where the platform allows it. A large PDF or image goes as the raw body of `POST printing/print/pdf` or `print/image` (`PrintPdfAsync` / `PrintImageAsync` taking a `Stream`, or `printPdf` / `printImage` taking a `Blob`), streamed to a temporary file, up to `PrintingBridgeOptions.MaxFileBytes` (512 MB). The JSON route is capped at `MaxJsonBytes` (16 MB). `POST printing/html-to-pdf` (`HtmlToPdfAsync`, or `htmlToPdf` in TypeScript) lays HTML out on pages and returns the PDF with no print UI, on iOS, Mac Catalyst and Android, where `capabilities.htmlToPdf` is true; elsewhere it answers `501`. Built on Shiny 5.9.0-beta-0009. Register them with `bridge.AddPrintersBridge()` and `bridge.AddPrintingBridge()`. The clients are `IPrintersBridge` (with a `PrintElements` builder) and `IPrintingBridge`, or `PrintersBridge` and `PrintingBridge` in TypeScript. Network printers work on every platform. Bluetooth LE printers work on Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS and Windows, and on Linux with Shiny.BluetoothLE.Linux registered. OS printing uses PrintManager on Android, AirPrint on iOS and Mac Catalyst, the spooler on Windows and CUPS on Linux and macOS. Content a platform can't print, such as HTML on Windows and CUPS, answers `501`, as does rendering on Linux without SkiaSharp.NativeAssets.Linux. See [Printing](/appdevicebridge/printing/).</RN>

<RN type="feature">**Live activities bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.LiveActivities`, backed by Shiny.Mobile.LiveActivities 5.9, adds `/_bridge/liveactivities`: iOS Live Activities on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and Android 16 Live Updates. The page starts, updates and ends activities with a title, body, short status, progress bar, stale date and data for a custom widget. An update can alert instead of refreshing silently. Push tokens, one per activity and the push-to-start token on iOS 17.2+, arrive as `liveactivities.token` and `liveactivities.starttoken`, in the page or in background.js when no page is open, so a server can update or start activities over APNs. Register it with `bridge.AddLiveActivitiesBridge()`, and on iOS set `ShinyLiveActivityWidget` to build Shiny's stock widget into the app. The client is `ILiveActivitiesBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.LiveActivities.Client`, or `LiveActivitiesBridge` in TypeScript. iOS 16.2+ and Android; every other platform answers `501`. See [Live Activities](/appdevicebridge/liveactivities/).</RN>
<RN type="fix">**AppLinks no longer crashes iOS and Mac Catalyst apps that use a scene delegate.** When the app launched without a link, the scene-connect handler read link and activity sets that UIKit leaves empty, and threw a `NullReferenceException`. Apps built with the iOS 27 SDK must use the UIScene life cycle, so every launch of such an app that registered `AddAppLinksBridge` hit it. Other platforms are unchanged.</RN>

## 1.1 - September 30, 2026

<RN type="breaking">**`Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Locations` is split into `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps` and `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Geofencing`**, following Shiny.Locations' split into Shiny.Gps and Shiny.Geofencing. `.Gps` has `AddGpsBridge()` and `AddMotionActivityBridge()`, and `.Geofencing` has `AddGeofenceBridge()`. Reference only the one you use. `AddLocationBridges()` is removed: call `AddGpsBridge().AddGeofenceBridge()` instead. The clients move with them. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps.Client` has `IGpsBridge`, `IMotionBridge` and `GpsJsonContext`, and `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Geofencing.Client` has `IGeofencesBridge` and `GeofencingJsonContext`. `LocationsJsonContext` is gone. `LocationAccessResult` becomes `GpsAccessResult`, `MotionAccessResult` and `GeofenceAccessResult`, with the same `{ "access": … }` JSON. In TypeScript, the `locations` module becomes `gps` and `geofencing`, still exported from the package root. Routes, events and handler names are unchanged. Platforms are unchanged: GPS and geofencing on Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows, motion activity on Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst, and `501` elsewhere.</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Beacons bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Beacons`, backed by Shiny.Beacons, adds `/_bridge/beacons`. Ranging reports the iBeacons in a region with estimated distances as `beacons.ranged`. Monitoring reports region enter and exit in the background and across restarts, as `beacons.region` and the `beacon` handler in background.js. Scanning reports Eddystone UID, URL, TLM and EID frames as `beacons.eddystone`, and broadcasting advertises the device as an iBeacon, Eddystone-UID or Eddystone-URL beacon. Register it with `bridge.AddBeaconsBridge()`, or pass `BeaconFeatures` flags for only some features. The client is `IBeaconsBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Beacons.Client`, or `BeaconsBridge` in TypeScript. Ranging and scans answer `409 not_listening` until their event is listened to, and stop with the last listener. Android, iOS, Mac Catalyst and Windows have every feature. macOS has Eddystone and broadcasting, and answers `501` for iBeacon ranging and monitoring, which CoreLocation lacks there. Linux answers `501`. See [Bridges](/appdevicebridge/bridges/).</RN>
