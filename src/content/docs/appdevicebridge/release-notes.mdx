## 2.3 TBD

<RN type="feature">**AI conversation bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.AiConversation`, backed by Shiny.AiConversation 3.0, adds `/_bridge/ai`: conversations with the app's own AI model, whose key, tools and prompts never reach the page. `POST ai/sessions` starts a stored, resumable session, and `GET ai/sessions/latest` carries on the most recent one. `POST ai/sessions/{id}/messages` sends text, an answer to a question with choices, images from file roots and per-turn instructions. The reply streams as `ai.delta` and ends with `ai.turn.ended`, or with `"wait": true` the request answers once the turn ends. A turn outlives the request that started it, a newer message supersedes it, and `DELETE ai/sessions/{id}/turn` interrupts it. The model's questions arrive as `ai.questions` with their choices. History pages backwards with each message's parts and image attachments, and `GET`/`PUT`/`DELETE ai/memory` manage what the assistant remembers. With `o => o.Voice = true`, `POST ai/voice` attaches the hands-free voice loop (wake word, push-to-talk, talking over a reply) to a session. It runs only while the page listens to `ai.voice.state`. Register it with `bridge.AddAiConversationBridge(ai => ai.SetChatClientProvider<T>())`. The client is `IAiConversationBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.AiConversation.Client`, or `AiConversationBridge` in TypeScript. Sessions run on every platform. The voice loop runs on iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, Android and Windows, and on Linux it needs a cloud or Whisper speech service; the bridge answers `501` for whatever isn't registered. This is a new security surface: a page that can reach the bridge spends the app's model and can lead it to call any tool the app registered. See [AI Conversation](/appdevicebridge/aiconversation/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Audio bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Audio`, backed by Shiny.Audio 3.0, adds `/_bridge/audio` for the device's speakers and microphone. `POST audio/playback` plays a clip from an absolute http or https URL or a file in a file root and answers with its `id`. Clips play together, `DELETE audio/playback/{id}` stops one, and `audio.playback.ended` says how each ended. `PUT audio/volume` sets the system media volume, and `audio.volume` reports every change. `POST audio/recording` records the microphone to 16 kHz WAV, optionally through an effect preset (`Robot`, `Chipmunk`, `DeepVoice`, `Cathedral`, `Telephone`, `Megaphone`, `Ensemble`) and keeping the clean take as well. `DELETE audio/recording` files it into the `data` root under `audio-recordings/`, and the app's `MaxRecordingDuration` (one hour by default) stops and files a recording left running. `GET audio/devices` lists the audio routes, and `POST audio/devices/picker` shows the system output picker. `POST audio/monitor` routes the microphone live to the output with adjustable gain. It runs only while the page listens to `audio.monitor.level`. A recording and the monitor share one microphone (`409 microphone_busy`). Register it with `bridge.AddAudioBridge()`. The client is `IAudioBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Audio.Client`, or `AudioBridge` in TypeScript. Playback, recording and routes run on iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, Android, Windows and Linux. The monitor runs on all of those except Windows, which answers `501`, and setting the volume answers `501` on iOS and Mac Catalyst. See [Audio](/appdevicebridge/audio/).</RN>
<RN type="enhancement">**Speech tone.** `POST speech/speak` takes an optional `tone`: an `emotion` (`Happy`, `Excited`, `Calm`, `Whispering`, and others), an `intensity` from 0 to 2, and free-text `instructions`. It passes through to Shiny.Speech's `SpeechTone`, so an expressive cloud engine registered in place of the on-device one (Azure, ElevenLabs v3, Typecast, OpenAI) delivers it, and the on-device engines ignore it. An intensity outside 0–2, or instructions over 1,000 characters, answers `400`. All platforms with a speech engine. See [Speech](/appdevicebridge/speech/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**NFC bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Nfc`, backed by the new Shiny.Nfc 6.5, adds `/_bridge/nfc` for reading and writing NDEF tags: URLs, text, MIME payloads, smart posters and Android app records. A WebView has no Web NFC, so this is the page's only way to reach the radio. `POST nfc/read` and `POST nfc/write` handle one tap. To keep scanning, listen for `nfc.tag`, which delivers each tag with its message already read, then `POST nfc/scan`. The scan ends with `DELETE nfc/scan`, when the page stops listening, or when the platform ends it (`nfc.scan`). Scanning with `holdSeconds` keeps each tag available to `POST nfc/tags/{tagId}/write` and `/lock` until the page releases it. Records to write are a URI, text, MIME, Android package or raw record. Tag failures answer with codes such as `410 tag_lost`, `409 tag_read_only` and `413 message_too_large`, and only one NFC session runs at a time (`409 nfc_busy`). Register it with `bridge.AddNfcBridge()`. The client is `INfcBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Nfc.Client`, or `NfcBridge` in TypeScript. Android, iOS, and PC/SC readers on Windows, macOS and Linux; Mac Catalyst and devices without NFC answer `501`. See [NFC](/appdevicebridge/nfc/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Biometrics bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Biometrics`, backed by the new Shiny.Biometrics 6.5, adds `/_bridge/biometrics`: the platform's own biometric prompt for the page. `GET biometrics` reports, with no UI, whether biometrics are available, enrolled or locked out, the hardware present (`Fingerprint`, `Face`, `Iris`, or `Unknown` for Windows Hello) and whether a passcode is set. `POST biometrics/authenticate` with a `reason` shows the prompt, and `allowDeviceCredential` lets the user use their passcode or PIN instead. A cancel, lockout or missing enrollment comes back as the result's `status` rather than an error. A missing `NSFaceIDUsageDescription` or Android activity answers `409 cannot_prompt`. Register it with `bridge.AddBiometricsBridge()`. The client is `IBiometricsBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Biometrics.Client`, or `BiometricsBridge` in TypeScript. iOS, Mac Catalyst and macOS (Touch ID, Face ID, Optic ID), Android (fingerprint, face, iris) and Windows (Windows Hello, which always offers its PIN); Linux answers `501`. The answer is a local yes/no for the page's UI, not proof of identity for a server. See [Biometrics](/appdevicebridge/biometrics/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Share target bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.ShareTarget` adds `/_bridge/sharetarget`, which makes the app a place other apps can share text, links and files to. Each share waits in a queue for the page: across restarts, until the page removes it, with the oldest dropped past `MaxPending` (20). Its files are copied into a file root (`data` by default) under `shares/{id}/` and read through the files bridge. `GET sharetarget/pending` lists the queue, `GET` and `DELETE sharetarget/pending/{id}` read or remove one share (removing deletes its files), and `sharetarget.received` fires while the page is open. A share's `url` is only ever an absolute http or https URL. Text that is nothing but such a link arrives as the `url`, and any other scheme is kept as text. File names are cleaned to one path segment. Register it with `bridge.AddShareTargetBridge()`. Platform code the bridge does not hook, such as a share extension or the AppKit app delegate, hands content over with `ShareTarget.ReceiveAsync`. The client is `IShareTargetBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.ShareTarget.Client`, or `ShareTargetBridge` in TypeScript. Android (send intents; `content:` files only), iOS and Mac Catalyst (files through "Open in" and "Copy to"; text and links need the app's own share extension), Windows (the Share Target contract and file activation), macOS through the app delegate, and Linux from the command line with `AcceptCommandLineFiles`. Without its file root, the bridge answers `501`. See [Share Target](/appdevicebridge/share-target/).</RN>

## 2.2 - October 27, 2026

<RN type="feature">**Age signals bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.AgeSignals`, backed by Shiny.AgeSignals 6.4, adds `/_bridge/agesignals`: the user's age range as the app store shares it, through Apple Declared Age Range and Google Play Age Signals, for app store accountability laws such as Texas SB 2420. `POST agesignals/request` with one to three ascending age gates answers the range, how it was established (`SelfDeclared`, `GuardianDeclared`, `Confirmed`, `IdVerified`), and whether it was shared, declined or needs verification. `GET agesignals` reports, with no UI, whether the OS has the API and Apple's eligibility and regulatory flags, and `POST agesignals/significant-update` shows Apple's significant-update sheet. Store failures answer with codes such as `503 network` and `403 app_not_owned`. Register it with `bridge.AddAgeSignalsBridge()`; Apple also needs the `com.apple.developer.declared-age-range` entitlement. The client is `IAgeSignalsBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.AgeSignals.Client`, or `AgeSignalsBridge` in TypeScript. iOS, macOS and Android; Mac Catalyst, Windows and Linux answer `501`. See [Age Signals](/appdevicebridge/agesignals/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**VPN bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Vpn`, backed by Shiny.Net.Vpn 6.4, adds `/_bridge/vpn` for the app's profile in the operating system's built-in IKEv2 VPN client. The page saves a profile authenticated by a shared secret, a username and password, or a PKCS#12 client certificate (the first save shows the OS consent prompt and answers `Denied` if the user declines), removes it, connects and waits for the tunnel, disconnects, and follows the tunnel through the `vpn.status` event, including changes made in system settings. `GET vpn` reports the state and what the platform can do, and an option it lacks answers `501`. Connection failures answer `502 vpn_connection_failed` with the OS's reason. Register it with `bridge.AddVpnBridge()`; Apple also needs the Personal VPN entitlement. The client is `IVpnBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Vpn.Client`, or `VpnBridge` in TypeScript. iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, Android 11+ (Android 13 to report the state) and Linux through NetworkManager's strongSwan plugin; Windows answers `501`. This is a new security surface: a page that can save a profile decides where the device's traffic goes, so register it only for a web app you ship. See [VPN](/appdevicebridge/vpn/).</RN>
<RN type="enhancement">**Shiny 6.4.** Every Shiny-backed bridge now builds on Shiny 6.4.0, up from 6.3.0. The Camera and Desktop bridges move to Shiny.Maui.Controls 2.1.0, whose desktop features register through one `UseShinyDesktop()` call. `AddTrayIconBridge()` and `AddQuickEntryBridge()` make that call for you, so app code is unchanged. The server builds on Shiny.Net.HttpServer 2.2.1 and the settings bridge on Shiny.Extensions.Stores 6.0.1.</RN>
<RN type="fix">**A Bluetooth LE read, write or notification cut off by a disconnect no longer reports an unobserved task exception.** When the page's request or subscription had already ended, the operation's late `BleException` surfaced through `TaskScheduler.UnobservedTaskException`, which an app forwarding those to crash telemetry logged as an error on every disconnect. It is now dropped. A request still waiting gets its error as before. From Shiny.BluetoothLE 6.3.1. All platforms. See [Bluetooth LE](/appdevicebridge/bluetoothle/).</RN>
<RN type="fix" platform="iOS">**An exception while Bluetooth LE handles an adapter state change or a state restoration no longer crashes the app.** CoreBluetooth calls these from `async void` callbacks, so an exception there ended the process; it is now logged. On Android, a connection-state subscriber that throws is logged too, instead of reporting an unobserved task exception. From Shiny.BluetoothLE 6.3.1. iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS and Android.</RN>
<RN type="fix">**A printer scan or sweep that fails after the page stopped listening no longer reports an unobserved task exception.** A network scan's mDNS browse or subnet sweep could fail after `printers/scan` had ended, and the failure went unobserved. It is now dropped. From Shiny.Printers 6.3.1. All platforms. See [Printing](/appdevicebridge/printing/).</RN>
<RN type="fix" platform="Android">**`DELETE notifications/{id}` removes a notification that was sent straight away and is still showing.** Shiny only stored scheduled, repeating and geofence notifications, so cancelling one sent with no schedule found nothing and left it in the notification shade. iOS already removed it. From Shiny.Notifications 6.4.0. See [Notifications](/appdevicebridge/notifications/).</RN>
<RN type="fix">**TypeScript: binary fields in a request body are base64 strings.** The generated clients typed a byte array inside a JSON body as `Blob | ArrayBuffer | Uint8Array`, but the body is sent with `JSON.stringify`, which writes those as `{}` or `{"0":…}`, so the bridge refused it. They are now `string`, carrying base64, as the native bridge reads them. This changes `BleWriteRequest.data`, so Bluetooth LE `write` from TypeScript now takes base64 (for example `btoa(String.fromCharCode(...bytes))`). Raw bodies, such as a file write or a PDF to print, still take binary.</RN>

<RN type="feature">**Game center bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.GameCenter`, backed by Shiny.GameCenter 6.3, adds `/_bridge/games` for Apple Game Center and Google Play Games. The page signs the player in, unlocks achievements, sets or increments their progress, reveals hidden ones, submits scores, and reads achievements, leaderboard scores (global or friends, today, this week or all time) and friends, and opens the services' own achievement and leaderboard screens. Unlocks, progress and scores are queued on the device and sent once the player is signed in and online, so they succeed offline; `GET games` reports `pendingCount` and `POST games/flush` sends the queue now. Achievements and leaderboards are named by the keys the app registers with `bridge.AddGameCenterBridge(o => o.AddAchievement(…).AddLeaderboard(…))`. `games.player` fires on sign-in, sign-out and account switches. Service failures answer with codes such as `401 not_authenticated`, `404 invalid_id` and `503 network`. The client is `IGameCenterBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.GameCenter.Client`, or `GameCenterBridge` in TypeScript. iOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS and Android; Windows and Linux answer `501`. See [Game Center](/appdevicebridge/gamecenter/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Call directory bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.CallDirectory`, backed by Shiny.Mobile.CallDirectory 6.3, adds `/_bridge/calldirectory` for call blocking and caller identification. The page adds, removes, replaces and clears a list of numbers to `Block`, `Identify` (ring with a label as caller ID) or `Allow` (Android: always ring, even when unlisted callers are blocked), reloads it into the system and reads how the load went, and turns numbers as people write them into the stored form with `POST calldirectory/normalize`. `GET calldirectory` reports whether the user turned the feature on and how many numbers it holds; `POST calldirectory/access` opens Settings on iOS and asks for the call screening role on Android. Register it with `bridge.AddCallDirectoryBridge()`; on iOS the app's project also sets `ShinyCallDirectoryExtension` and `ShinyCallDirectoryAppGroup`. The client is `ICallDirectoryBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.CallDirectory.Client`, or `CallDirectoryBridge` in TypeScript. iOS and Android 10+; every other platform answers `501`. See [Call Directory](/appdevicebridge/calldirectory/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Receipt printer status and subnet sweeps.** `GET printers/connection/status` (`GetPrinterStatusAsync()`, `getPrinterStatus()` in TypeScript) reads the connected printer's sensors: online, cover open, paper near its end or out, cutter and other errors, the cash-drawer pin, and `canPrint`. It works for ESC/POS printers on the network, which `ConnectedPrinter.supportsStatus` reports; other printers answer `501`, and one that doesn't reply answers `504 printer_timeout`. A network scan with `sweep: true` also finds printers that don't advertise over mDNS by trying a TCP connection to every host on the device's subnets on the allowed printer ports, and each network result's `discovery` says how it was found. Built on Shiny.Printers 6.3. All platforms. See [Printing](/appdevicebridge/printing/#printer-status).</RN>
<RN type="enhancement">**Notification actions that require unlocking.** A channel action's `requiresUnlock` makes the user unlock the device before the action runs, for actions with a real-world effect. iOS, Mac Catalyst and macOS, and Android 12 and later; older Android versions run the action without asking, and Windows and Linux ignore it. In TypeScript, `NotificationChannelAction` now has a required `requiresUnlock` field. See [Notifications](/appdevicebridge/notifications/#channels-and-actions).</RN>
<RN type="enhancement">**`applicationIdentifier` in `GET app/info`**, from Shiny.Core's `IPlatform`: the bundle identifier on Apple, the package name on Android, the package identity name on a packaged Windows app, or the Flatpak app id or Snap name on Linux, and `null` when the app has none. All platforms.</RN>

## 2.0 - October 12, 2026

<RN type="breaking">**Shiny 6.** Every Shiny-backed bridge now builds on Shiny 6.1.1. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Maui` starts Shiny's MAUI host (`UseShiny()`) on every head, including the macOS (AppKit) and Linux (GTK4) heads, which Shiny.Hosting.Maui 6 now ships. The bridges no longer register Shiny's core services for themselves on macOS. `UseAppDeviceBridge` also registers Shiny.Core's `IBattery` and `IConnectivity`. HttpTransfers on Linux uses Shiny.Core's connectivity, so it no longer depends on `Shiny.Core.Linux`.</RN>
<RN type="breaking">**`Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.AppSupport`, `.AppSupport.Client` and `.AppSupport.Linux` are removed.** The app support bridge is split in two. **`/_bridge/app`** moves into `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge` itself, over Shiny.Core and Shiny.AppStore, and is registered with `bridge.AddAppBridge(store, startup)`. It serves info, orientation, the screen, settings, the store, launch at login, battery and connectivity, with no MAUI dependency. **`/_bridge/device`** is new in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Maui`, over .NET MAUI Essentials, and is registered with `bridge.AddDeviceBridge()`. It takes the browser, maps, sharing, haptics, vibration, keep-awake and the clipboard, so `app/browser`, `app/map`, `app/share`, `app/haptics`, `app/vibrate` and `app/clipboard` become `device/…`, and `app/screen/keep-awake` becomes `device/keep-awake`. The sensors bridge moves to `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Maui` with `bridge.AddSensorsBridge()`; its routes are unchanged. `IAppBridge`, `IDeviceBridge` and `ISensorsBridge` and their contracts are now in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Client`, registered with `AddAppBridgeClient()`, `AddDeviceBridgeClient()` and `AddSensorsBridgeClient()`. In TypeScript, the `app-support` module is gone: `AppBridge`, `DeviceBridge` and `SensorsBridge` come from the package root as before. Contract changes: the browser and map calls return `DeviceActionResult`. `ScreenInfo` drops `rotation` and `keepScreenOn`, and its `refreshRate` is a double. `app.battery` now carries `BatteryInfo`, energy saver included, and `app.energysaver` is removed. `AppStoreOptions` and `StartupServiceOptions` become Shiny.AppStore's `AppStoreOptions` and Shiny.Core's `AppStartupOptions`. `AddAppSupportLinux()` is removed: the Linux (GTK4) head reads the battery from Shiny.Core's sysfs implementation, with change events. Platforms are unchanged; each feature still answers `501` where its platform lacks it. See [App & Device](/appdevicebridge/app/).</RN>
<RN type="breaking">**`Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.DocumentGeofencing` is replaced by `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.RegionGeofencing`**, following Shiny.DocumentDb.Geofencing and Shiny.DocumentDb.Geo's move into Shiny 6.1 as Shiny.Geofencing.Regions and Shiny.Geofencing.Regions.DocumentDb. `AddDocumentGeofenceBridge(cfg => cfg.AddRegionSet<T>(…))` becomes `AddRegionGeofenceBridge(cfg => cfg.AddDocumentRegionSet<T>(…))`, and the reference dataset's sets are `cfg.AddNorthAmericanRegions()` and `cfg.AddNorthAmericanCities()`, seeded by `services.AddNorthAmericaSeeder()` with the store mapped by `opts.MapNorthAmericaData()`. The routes move from `/_bridge/documentgeofences` to `/_bridge/regiongeofences`, the event from `documentgeofence.change` to `regiongeofence.change`, and the background.js handler from `documentgeofence` to `regiongeofence`. The client is `IRegionGeofencesBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.RegionGeofencing.Client` (`AddRegionGeofencesBridgeClient()`), or `RegionGeofencesBridge` in TypeScript. Its contracts drop the `Document` prefix (`RegionChange`, `CurrentRegion`, `RegionGeofenceStatus`, `RegionGeofencingJsonContext`), and `RegionSetInfo` no longer carries `withinMeters`. `WebAppDocumentGeofenceDelegate` and `WebAppDocumentGeofenceOptions` become `WebAppRegionGeofenceDelegate` and `WebAppRegionGeofenceOptions`. `region` is no longer written through the document store's options by default: set `o.RegionSerializerOptions` to the store's `JsonSerializerOptions`. `start` no longer answers `501 spatial_not_supported`, because a store without spatial queries now fails when the app starts. Platforms are unchanged: Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst, and `501` elsewhere. See [Region Geofencing](/appdevicebridge/region-geofencing/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Geocoder and custom region sets.** Besides documents, the region geofencing bridge monitors sets resolved by reverse geocoding (`cfg.AddGeocoderRegionSet("states", GeocoderRegionLevel.AdministrativeArea)`: country, state or province, county, city, neighbourhood or postal code, with no data to ship) and sets from any `IRegionSource` (`cfg.AddRegionSet(name, sp => …)`). A geocoder set's `region` is the placemark, written without any serializer options. Android, iOS and Mac Catalyst. See [Region Geofencing](/appdevicebridge/region-geofencing/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**`app.screen`, `POST app/store/review-page` and `GET device/keep-awake`.** `app.screen` fires with the new `ScreenInfo` whenever the main display changes (a rotation, a resolution or scale change, or another display), from Shiny.Core's `IScreenInfo`. `store/review-page` opens the store listing on its reviews, through Shiny.AppStore's `OpenReviewPage`. `device/keep-awake` reports whether the screen is being kept on. All platforms Shiny.Core and Shiny.AppStore support; `501` where there is no store registered.</RN>
<RN type="fix">**Keeping the screen on where the platform cannot is a `501`, not a silent success.** `PUT device/keep-awake` reads the value back after setting it. A backend that drops it, such as Essentials on plain .NET, now answers `501` instead of `204`. Platforms that implement it are unchanged.</RN>

## 1.2 - October 9, 2026

<RN type="feature">**Database bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Database` adds `/_bridge/database`, a database client on the device for SQLite files. It opens SQLite files in the app's file roots where they are, named by the same `{ root, path }` as the files bridge. A page reads the schema (columns, indexes, foreign keys, checks and triggers). It runs scripts with every result set, per-statement messages, the error's line, `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` as a tree, and cancel by `runId`. It pages, sorts, filters, searches, counts and totals a table, and inserts, updates and deletes rows by key. It previews the DDL for a table design, including SQLite's rebuild, and drops or renames objects. It imports CSV in one transaction and exports a table or a statement's answer to a file in a root. Query history and saved queries are kept per database in the app's data directory. Statements are interrupted after `DatabaseBridgeOptions.StatementTimeout` (30 seconds). Security: queries can write, but SQLite's authorizer refuses `ATTACH` (and so `VACUUM INTO`) and `load_extension`, so a query cannot reach a file outside the file roots or load native code. Other engines plug in through `IDatabaseDriver` (`services.AddDatabaseDriver<T>()`), which serves a connection the page names by `{ connection, database }`; `GET database/connections` lists them and `POST database/databases` lists a connection's databases. Register the bridge with `bridge.AddDatabaseBridge()`. The client is `IDatabaseBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Database.Client`, or `DatabaseBridge` in TypeScript. All platforms. See [Database](/appdevicebridge/database/).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Pluggable map, router and geocoder providers, with Azure Maps and Google Maps built in.** The maps bridge's online services are now providers on `MapsOptions`, the way traffic already was, so the same page draws OpenStreetMap, Azure Maps or Google Maps. `o.Basemap` takes an `IBasemapProvider`: `ProtomapsBasemapProvider` (OpenStreetMap vector tiles, as before), `AzureMapsBasemapProvider` (Render tiles: road, dark grey, imagery) or `GoogleMapsBasemapProvider` (Map Tiles API 2D tiles: roadmap, satellite, terrain). Raster maps are served at the new `GET /_bridge/maps/basemap/{z}/{x}/{y}`, and `<BridgeMap>` lays them over downloaded regions, which show through offline. `o.Directions.Router` takes an `IRouteProvider`: `ValhallaRouteProvider`, `AzureMapsRouteProvider` (with live traffic) or `GoogleMapsRouteProvider`. `GET /_bridge/directions` reports the router's `onlineModes`, a mode it lacks answers `400 mode_unsupported`, and every route carries its `attribution`. `AzureMapsGeocoder` and `GoogleMapsGeocoder` join `NominatimGeocoder`. One `AzureMapsCredential` (a shared key or Microsoft Entra ID) serves every Azure provider. Keys stay in the app, and each provider can be swapped while the app runs. All platforms. See [Providers](/appdevicebridge/maps/#providers).</RN>
<RN type="breaking">**Maps options are providers.** `MapsOptions.OnlineTiles` and `OnlineMaxZoom` are replaced by `o.Basemap = new ProtomapsBasemapProvider(url) { MaxZoom }`, whose `ConfigureRequest` now carries tile headers (`MapsOptions.ConfigureRequest` covers the catalog, downloads, glyphs and sprites). `DirectionsOptions.OnlineRouteUrl`, `ApiKey` and `ConfigureRequest` are replaced by `o.Directions.Router = new ValhallaRouteProvider(url) { ApiKey, ConfigureRequest }`. `MapsInfo.Online` is replaced by `MapsInfo.Basemap`. `TrafficTileFormat` is now `TileFormat` and `TrafficTile` is now `ProviderTile`, shared by every tile provider. `AzureMapsTrafficProvider(clientId, getAccessToken)` becomes `AzureMapsTrafficProvider(new AzureMapsCredential(clientId, getAccessToken))`.</RN>
<RN type="feature">**Reverse geocoding bridge.** `AddGeocodingBridge()` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps` adds `GET /_bridge/geocoding/reverse?latitude=&longitude=`, which returns the placemarks at a position, most relevant first. It uses MapKit / CoreLocation on iOS and Mac Catalyst and `android.location.Geocoder` on Android. Windows, Linux, macOS and Android devices without a geocoding backend use OpenStreetMap's Nominatim (Shiny.Gps 5.9.0-beta-0009), so it works on every platform; register `AddGeocoding(o => …)` first to point it at your own server. A position off the map answers `400`, and an unreachable geocoder `503 geocoder_unavailable`. The client is `IGeocodingBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Gps.Client`, or `GeocodingBridge` in TypeScript. See [GPS & Motion](/appdevicebridge/gps/#reverse-geocoding).</RN>
<RN type="feature">**In-app purchases bridge.** `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.InAppPurchases`, backed by Shiny.Mobile.InAppPurchases 5.9, adds `/_bridge/purchases` for consumables, non-consumables and auto-renewable subscriptions through the App Store (StoreKit 2) and Google Play Billing. The page loads localized products with their subscription offers, opens the store's purchase sheet (`Success`, `Pending`, `Cancelled` and `AlreadyOwned` are results, not errors), and reads entitlements and unfinished purchases. It can also restore purchases and open subscription management. `POST purchases/transactions/{transactionId}/finish` with `{ consume }` finishes a purchase once the app's server has verified `verificationData` and granted it. The bridge looks the purchase up in the store by its transaction id instead of trusting a record from the page, and answers `404 purchase_not_found` when there is none. Ask to Buy approvals, renewals, refunds and purchases on other devices arrive as `purchases.updated`, in the page or in background.js when no page is open. Store failures answer with codes the page can switch on, such as `503 store_unavailable`, `403 not_allowed`, `404 product_not_found` and `409 invalid_state`. Register it with `bridge.AddInAppPurchasesBridge()`; an app's own `IPurchaseDelegate` keeps running beside the bridge's. The client is `IInAppPurchasesBridge` in `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.InAppPurchases.Client`, or `InAppPurchasesBridge` in TypeScript. iOS 15+ and Android; every other platform answers `501`. <RN type="feature">**Printing bridges.** Two new packages, one for each of Shiny 5.9's printing models. `Shiny.AppDeviceBridge.Printers` adds `/_bridge/printers` for ESC/POS and TSPL receipt and label printers, backed by Shiny.Printers.
